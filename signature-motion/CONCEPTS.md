# Signature Motion: creative catalog

Six starter implementations establish a reusable visual language for agent-directed video. Twelve additional concepts describe a possible next collection; they are **planned designs, not implemented templates**.

Every current sample uses fictional demonstration copy and values. The uses below are potential integrations for a video editor and research agent; application connectors are not included. Use the README and example configurations for the implemented input contract; suggested future inputs are design requirements.

## What makes this collection work

Composition creates the first impression: one dominant subject, a clear reading order, deliberate empty space, and a stable place for supporting information. Typography carries meaning through scale, weight, line breaks, and timing. Animation should reveal that structure. Changing a gradient or easing curve cannot rescue an overloaded frame.

Each template therefore has a specific editorial job. The agent should select it from the story, available evidence, and assets. A claim needs a source; a trend needs a series; a workflow needs ordered steps. Brand colors are a later decision. Keep the final state readable long enough to absorb the message, and compose portrait and landscape separately where their spatial demands differ.

The timing examples below use a **12-second reference edit**. They communicate intended pacing; exact timings and supported parameters remain defined by the current renderer. Longer copy needs more reading time, not merely smaller type.

## Starter implementations

### 01. TYPE IMPACT — make one sentence matter

**Intent:** A bold editorial opener or closing statement. Oversized type, a measured rule, and a compact call to action create authority without relying on footage.

**Sequence:** 0–3s headline lines rise through masks; 3–5s the rule extends and supporting copy appears; 7–9s the call to action settles; the remaining time holds the complete composition. Small registration marks balance the larger type.

**Editable content:** Headline lines, subhead, call to action, brand, palette, duration, and aspect dimensions. Use short phrases and intentional breaks rather than a paragraph broken arbitrarily into rows.

**Possible use:** the video editor launch hooks, chapter openers, or end cards. the research agent-derived findings could supply the headline once supported by actual research.

**Priority:** Broadest reusable component; develop additional typographic compositions before adding decorative effects.

### 02. REDLINE — make evidence the visual subject

**Intent:** An editorial evidence card with paper texture, a source label, and a marker-style reveal. The visual hierarchy distinguishes the claim, the excerpt, and the takeaway.

**Sequence:** 0–3s the excerpt card straightens and settles beneath the headline; 3–6s highlight strokes move across its lines; 7–9s the verdict appears below; 9–12s holds the argument and attribution together.

**Editable content:** Headline, exact excerpt, source text, verdict, brand, palette, and duration. Keep the quotation short enough to read comfortably.

**Possible use:** A the research agent research finding becomes an evidence scene inside a the video editor explainer. Supplying a source label alone does not verify a claim; preserve the actual source in the surrounding editorial workflow.

**Priority:** A distinctive research-to-video bridge with modest asset requirements.

### 03. SIGNAL — let a real series tell the story

**Intent:** A restrained chart scene with a progressive line reveal, direct labels, a final-value annotation, and a concise interpretation.

**Sequence:** 0–2s establish headline, axes, and labels; 2–7s draw the series; 7–9s identify the final value and reveal the takeaway; 9–12s hold the complete chart and source.

**Editable content:** Headline, labeled numerical observations, unit, takeaway, source, palette, and brand. The series should contain comparable measurements with a clearly understood order.

**Possible use:** the video editor reports and performance recaps; the research agent research summaries when genuine quantitative evidence is available.

**Priority:** Valuable when the agent has structured data. Do not transform qualitative opinions into invented values or imply that fictional samples show actual results.

### 04. FLOW STATE — turn scattered work into a process

**Intent:** A visual transition from fragmented tasks to an ordered sequence. The satisfying moment is the alignment: separate pieces become a coherent path.

**Sequence:** 0–3s scattered task elements establish friction; 3–7s elements move toward organized positions; 7–9s the workflow resolves; 9–12s the final message holds beside the clarified structure.

**Editable content:** Workflow labels and supporting message, plus the brand and visual configuration supported by the example. Keep labels parallel: actions should use consistent grammatical forms.

**Possible use:** Explain a proposed the video editor editing pipeline or a the research agent research process. The animation illustrates a workflow; it does not prove that each stage executed.

**Priority:** Strong explanatory companion to the typographic opener. Future versions can distinguish completed, pending, and proposed steps.

### 05. PRISM — give supplied media a stage

**Intent:** A layered media deck with spatial offsets and controlled depth. Images become a composed presentation rather than a flat slideshow.

**Sequence:** 0–3s establish the deck; 3–7s offset and reveal its layers; 7–9s emphasize the principal media panel; 9–12s hold the visual and its supporting title.

**Editable content:** Supported local media, title or captions, palette, brand, and timing. Choose images with compatible crops and clear subjects.

**Possible use:** the video editor portfolios, visual references, or campaign showcases. the research agent could provide approved research illustrations when appropriate assets are supplied.

**Priority:** Expands the pack beyond text. This is a 2.5D composition: planar media does not provide unseen object surfaces or a true volumetric product model.

### 06. AFTER HOURS — build an atmosphere around a title

**Intent:** A bold title surrounded by orbital or iris-like geometry. The composition suits event identity, creative announcements, and short branded interstitials.

**Sequence:** 0–3s geometric motion establishes the atmosphere; 3–7s the title becomes the visual anchor; 7–10s surrounding shapes sustain energy; 10–12s completes the title treatment.

**Editable content:** Title and supporting copy, brand, palette, duration, and dimensions as exposed in the example. Favor a compact title with a strong silhouette.

**Possible use:** the video editor showreels, event openers, music-related visuals, or an the motion engine style demonstration.

**Priority:** A useful expressive counterpoint to the editorial scenes. Treat seamless playback as a separate requirement to verify before using any output as a continuous loop.

## Planned next collection

These concepts require new rendering logic, assets, or contracts. Their storyboards describe intended behavior rather than features in the starter pack.

### 07. PARALLEL UNIVERSE — the same task, two outcomes

**Storyboard:** 0–2s establish a shared starting state; 2–4s split into aligned versions; 4–8s compare matching milestones; 8–10s bring the result across the divider; 10–12s hold the measured difference.

**Agent inputs:** Paired clips or structured steps, alignment anchors, timing map, labels, real metrics, and a conclusion.

**Fit / priority:** High priority for the video editor comparisons and the research agent process explainers. Match the underlying task fairly. Label simulated or compressed sequences; animation speed is not measured productivity.

### 08. EXPLODED VIEW — reveal the layers of a product

**Storyboard:** 0–2s hero reveal; 2–5s separate supplied layers; 5–8s show three sequential callouts; 8–10s reassemble; 10–12s hold the product and benefit.

**Agent inputs:** Layered artwork, masks, or a genuine 3D asset; callouts; camera and lighting choices.

**Fit / priority:** High priority for the video editor product films. Layered UI can explain software architecture visually. A single photograph supports limited 2.5D movement, not a truthful orbit around unseen surfaces.

### 09. SIGNAL ATLAS — navigate a field of ideas

**Storyboard:** 0–2s show a central topic; 2–5s reveal related clusters; 5–8s focus on a selected region; 8–10s expose questions or opportunities; 10–12s resolve into a brief.

**Agent inputs:** Topics, explicit relationships, clusters, selection rationale, and optional sourced measurements.

**Fit / priority:** High priority for the research agent-to-the video editor research narratives. Without quantitative data, use equal-size markers and identify the view as a concept map. Size, position, and color must have understandable meanings.

### 10. DOMINO ENGINE — make causality tangible

**Storyboard:** 0–2s introduce an input token; 2–7s move it through three transformations; 7–9s assemble an output; 9–12s pull back to the completed route.

**Agent inputs:** Ordered stages, input/output types, actual branch choices, icons, and a final artifact.

**Fit / priority:** High priority after Flow State: richer explanations of research, editing, or automation. Mechanical, ceramic, and paper variants can share the same story structure. Animate only supplied causal relationships; distinguish proposed actions from completed work.

### 11. BLUEPRINT BLOOM — from outline to finished form

**Storyboard:** 0–3s draw supplied outlines; 3–5s place annotations; 5–9s transition through an aligned reveal into the finished image; 9–12s hold the result.

**Agent inputs:** Outline artwork, final media, registration anchors, annotations, and reveal direction.

**Fit / priority:** Medium priority for the video editor architecture, hardware, or interface showcases. The reveal depends on accurate alignment. Decorative lines should not be presented as authentic technical plans unless they actually are.

### 12. THE RECEIPT — make the total the payoff

**Storyboard:** 0–2s a receipt begins printing; 2–7s line items appear rhythmically; 7–9s calculate and emphasize the total; 9–12s tear away to an end card.

**Agent inputs:** Items, amounts, units, dates, source references, and an explicit total rule.

**Fit / priority:** High priority as an inexpensive, recognizable the video editor format for deliverables, pricing, or measured results. Validate arithmetic and comparable units. Time savings require real evidence rather than attractive invented totals.

### 13. HUMAN FOOTNOTES — annotate a person’s ideas

**Storyboard:** 0–3s establish a speaker; 3–7s add timed marginal notes; 7–9s enlarge a key quotation; 9–12s return focus to the speaker and conclusion.

**Agent inputs:** Footage, transcript timings, exact quotations, explanatory notes, and face-safe regions.

**Fit / priority:** High priority for the video editor interviews and founder stories. the research agent can supply verified contextual notes. Preserve the speaker’s meaning, maintain attribution, and place annotations away from faces and existing captions.

### 14. BILLBOARD TAKEOVER — place a campaign in a world

**Storyboard:** 0–3s establish a supplied scene; 3–6s activate one display; 6–9s extend the campaign across adjacent surfaces; 9–12s settle on the primary message.

**Agent inputs:** Background plate, screen quadrilaterals, campaign assets, copy, and optional tracking data.

**Fit / priority:** Medium priority for the video editor campaign presentations. A still plate permits a fixed view or digital pan; a moving camera requires suitable tracking. Keep campaign mockups clearly identifiable as proposed placements.

### 15. TIME MACHINE — make change legible

**Storyboard:** 0–2s establish a present-day object; 2–8s move through dated historical states in a stable frame; 8–10s return to the present; 10–12s explain the change.

**Agent inputs:** Dated media, timeline entries, alignment anchors, captions, and a supported interpretation.

**Fit / priority:** Medium priority for the video editor company histories or the research agent historical explainers. Use supplied historical material and explicit dates. Do not invent intermediate states or make unequal time intervals appear equivalent without explanation.

### 16. ONE-TAKE PORTAL — connect scenes through composition

**Storyboard:** 0–3s push toward a chosen region in scene A; 3–6s reveal scene B inside it; 6–9s repeat one matched transition; 9–12s arrive at a hero frame.

**Agent inputs:** Ordered media, paired crop rectangles, masks, focal points, and transition timing.

**Fit / priority:** Medium priority for the video editor travel, culture, and product worlds. Its quality depends on compatible compositions. Promise a controlled compositing transition, not physically continuous footage generated from missing views.

### 17. SOUNDFORM — let audio shape the identity

**Storyboard:** 0–2s establish cover art and title; 2–8s animate a sculptural ribbon or type field from audio features; 8–10s emphasize a musical event; 10–12s resolve the identity.

**Agent inputs:** Audio, measured amplitude or frequency features, artwork, title, palette, and response limits.

**Fit / priority:** Medium priority for the video editor podcasts, music, and event visuals. Motion should derive from supplied audio. Limit flashing and avoid large full-frame brightness changes on every beat.

### 18. DECISION CHAMBER — show why one option wins

**Storyboard:** 0–2s state a decision; 2–7s compare two or three candidates against visible criteria; 7–9s advance the chosen option; 9–12s hold the rationale and principal tradeoff.

**Agent inputs:** Candidates, criteria, actual scores or qualitative assessments, sources, recommendation, and uncertainty.

**Fit / priority:** High priority for the research agent comparison research assembled in the video editor. Visual weight must follow the supplied assessment. Preserve unknowns and tradeoffs; animation must not create a ranking unsupported by the evidence.

## Build order and selection

First expand the most reusable story structures: **Parallel Universe, Receipt, Human Footnotes, and Decision Chamber**. Then build the more asset-dependent **Exploded View** and **Signal Atlas**. Domino Engine becomes stronger once the workflow contract is established; the remaining concepts broaden the visual range.

For agent selection, record each template’s story role, required inputs, content limits, supported aspect layouts, duration range, and asset requirements. Missing information should lead to a suitable alternative: no numerical series means no chart; no layered artwork means a simpler media composition. Keep styles interchangeable while preserving the underlying story.
